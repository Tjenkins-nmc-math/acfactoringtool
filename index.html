The AC Method Explained

The AC Method (also called Factoring by Grouping) breaks down trinomials of the form $ax^2 + bx + c$ into four terms so they can easily be factored. Here is the step-by-step procedure:

Step 1

Multiply $a \cdot c$

Identify coefficients $a$, $b$, and $c$. Calculate the key product $M = a \cdot c$.

Step 2

Find the Magic Factor Pair

Find two integers $m$ and $n$ such that:

  • $m \cdot n = ac$
  • $m + n = b$
Step 3

Split the Middle Term

Rewrite $bx$ using $m$ and $n$ to expand the trinomial into 4 terms:

$ax^2 + mx + nx + c$

Step 4 & 5

Factor by Grouping

Group the first two terms and the last two terms. Factor out the GCF from each binomial group to reach the binomial pair answer $(px + q)(rx + s)$.

Unfactorable (Prime) Trinomials

When the AC Method yields no integer factor pair

Not every quadratic trinomial $ax^2 + bx + c$ can be factored into linear binomials with integer coefficients. If you exhaust all integer factor pairs of $a \cdot c$ and none of them sum to $b$, the trinomial cannot be factored using the AC method and is classified as prime over integers (unfactorable).

Example: Is $x^2 + 3x + 5$ factorable?

  1. Identify coefficients: $a = 1$, $b = 3$, $c = 5$.
  2. Calculate key product: $a \cdot c = 1 \cdot 5 = 5$.
  3. List all integer factor pairs of $5$ and test their sums:
    • $1 \times 5 = 5 \implies 1 + 5 = 6 \quad (\neq 3)$
    • $(-1) \times (-5) = 5 \implies (-1) + (-5) = -6 \quad (\neq 3)$
  4. Conclusion: Because no integer factor pair of $ac = 5$ adds up to $b = 3$, the trinomial $x^2 + 3x + 5$ is prime (unfactorable over integers).
